> [!quote] Field Note
> A plan is easier to trust when the people who must use it can question and shape it.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use four examples to test whether people can challenge a plan, shape the work, and show how a promise will be used.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before closing a decision, ask whose experience is missing. Give that person a clear chance to challenge an assumption and explain what would change the plan.

A calm meeting can leave hard questions untouched. HBR's Rebecca Newton describes senior teams that avoid dissent out of respect or fear of slowing decisions. She argues for raising and testing opposing ideas.

A Kauffman Foundation account offers a related example. Lou Smith recalls Ewing Kauffman asking a quiet board member to speak because others needed to learn from him. The account also describes why an offer of education needed support around it.

Two other sources extend the question of who contributes. AACSB's Global Impact Awards name leadership, research, societal impact, and teaching as distinct areas. The World Bank's new Philanthropy Hub aims to connect capital, knowledge, and partners.

These sources do not prove one leadership method. They offer advice, recollections, program descriptions, and stated aims. The practical test is yours: who can challenge the plan, who helps carry it out, and what evidence would show that it serves its users?

> **Use today:** Make one listening or review practice part of the work, with time and responsibility assigned to it.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Identify one assumption in a group plan. Ask a teammate with a different role what experience could challenge it.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Invite a missing perspective before closing a decision. State who may change the plan and how the team will respond to dissent.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Name the people who should use a project and invite them to test its promised value. Define evidence of use beyond the finished output.
