## The Field Note

Installing a security fix and closing an investigation are different decisions. A team needs a clear record of which question it has answered.

On November 13, CISA added two vulnerabilities to its catalog of known exploited vulnerabilities. That means the agency has evidence of active exploitation. Its notice points to federal requirements that prioritize high-risk, publicly exposed systems and include checks for compromise before repair.

Those binding rules apply to U.S. federal civilian executive branch agencies. The notice does not prove that your system is affected or compromised. Other organizations must check their own products, exposure, and relevant guidance.

Ask the responsible security team to keep three questions separate. Is the affected product present? Is it exposed? What checks for compromise does the applicable guidance require? Record the repair status and the investigation status separately.

> [!example] Try this
> Ask the responsible security team to record whether the affected product is present, whether it is exposed, and whether compromise checks are required. Keep remediation and investigation status separate.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> When documenting a technical fix, separate the change you made from the evidence that the original problem is resolved.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Ask who owns exposure assessment, remediation, and incident investigation, and make the handoffs explicit.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Use a fictional incident to have learners distinguish vulnerability, exposure, exploitation evidence, and confirmed compromise.

## Reading Room

**Paper:** Jason R. C. Nurse (2025), To Patch or Not to Patch: Motivations, Challenges, and Implications for Cybersecurity, Lecture Notes in Computer Science, pp. 265–281. [Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-92833-8_16).

Nurse synthesizes research and industry literature on why organizations install security patches or delay them. The abstract names resource limits, manual work, human error, and unreliable tools. It also discusses faulty patches, business needs, and rules. This account is based on the abstract. This synthesis does not establish the safest sequence for a particular system. Name the reason for delay and assign an owner to resolve it. A patching plan should record the operational constraint, the exposure assessment, and the next review date without treating delay as evidence of safety.
