## The Field Note

> [!example] Know the Edge
> Before you act, name the evidence boundary, the skill boundary, and the person closest to the missing knowledge.

Today's sources point to a simple discipline: know the edge before you act.

A business survey can show conditions across many firms. It cannot decide for one firm without local evidence. A founder course can make risk easier to discuss. It cannot remove the risk. A community-development report can show a better design path. It cannot prove every project will shift power. A GAO recommendation can name a public-agency gap. It cannot close the gap by itself.

The better move is not to ignore the evidence. It is to use the evidence inside its real boundary.

Try this three-part check:

1. **Evidence:** What does the source actually prove?
2. **Competence:** What do we know how to judge well?
3. **Local truth:** Who sees the missing condition before we do?

That question protects students from turning sources into slogans. It protects founders from treating broad data as a command. It protects managers from asking a dashboard to do a conversation's job. It protects public and community leaders from mistaking a recommendation for completed work.

The limit matters. These sources do not prove one rule works in every business, classroom, community, or agency. They do support a useful practice: name the edge before the edge makes the decision for you.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Before using a source as advice, write one sentence that says what it does not prove.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Split one decision into three parts: broad signal, local evidence, and the judgment you are qualified to make.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to name the boundary of their evidence before they make a recommendation.
