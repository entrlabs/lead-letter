## The Field Note

> [!example] Trust is a practice of checking
> Before accepting a promise, name the risk, the reviewer, the evidence, and the consequence that still needs testing.

Inside Higher Ed reports that the Department of Defense asked 30 universities to audit partnerships connected to institutions on the Section 1286 list. The report describes reviews that affected some study-abroad, exchange, and dual-degree relationships. An audit can show that a process occurred. It does not show that every partnership was unsafe or that the policy improves research security.

Temple University's decision to terminate a reported $55 million gift shows the same governance problem from another direction. Resources carry obligations. A university must assess the relationship, the allegations, the evidence, and the authority for the decision. The report does not determine guilt or quantify the eventual institutional loss.

AACSB's case-teaching workshop makes the routine practical. Good discussion depends on preparation, participation, response management, and adaptation. The World Bank Group's auditor-general appointment offers a governance parallel: independent assurance is meant to test controls and risk claims for leaders and governing bodies. Farnam Street's leadership lens adds discipline and focus, but it is a practitioner reflection, not causal evidence.

Use the sequence when a decision carries institutional risk: define the claim, name who checks it, preserve the evidence trail, and state what remains unknown. A completed review is a process outcome. It is not proof that the underlying risk has disappeared.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Separate the reported rule, review, and decision from the outcome that still needs evidence.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Map one high-stakes decision to its risk owner, reviewer, evidence trail, and unresolved consequence.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Design a case around the decision learners must make, the evidence they should use, and the feedback that tests the next attempt.
