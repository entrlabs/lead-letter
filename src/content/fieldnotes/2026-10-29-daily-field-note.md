> [!quote] Field Note
> Before you add scale, find the failure that would make the promise unusable.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use one inversion question to expose the weak link in a plan before adding more tools, money, or people.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Try this
> Ask: “What would make this plan fail even if the main idea is right?” Name the earliest visible warning and assign one person to check it.

The World Bank’s October 28 commodity outlook describes connected shocks across energy, food, fertilizer, and supply. HBR’s fraud analysis warns that AI deception can exploit gaps between teams. Individual systems may still look secure. Kauffman describes grant support for Guadalupe Centers’ workforce programs, which connect workers and employers.

These sources do not prove one universal rule. The outlook is a forecast. The articles offer expert views and a program description, not proof of results. They support one practical move: inspect the connection that carries the promise before adding more resources. Choose one link: a supplier, an approval, or a partner. Write how its failure would affect the people relying on your plan. Set a warning sign and a check date before expanding.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Inversion and The Power of Avoiding Stupidity](https://fs.blog/inversion/) (publication date not stated)
> **Useful idea:** Think about what would make the desired result fail, then remove the most obvious preventable condition.
> **Use today:** Before a project launch, write one failure case, one early warning sign, and one preventive action.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> What assumption would make your project unusable even if your idea is sound?

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Which handoff, decision, or control could fail quietly as the team grows?

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to state the strongest failure case before they defend the preferred answer.
