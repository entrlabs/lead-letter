> Capacity becomes usable when people align the system, explain its standards, and test its assumptions.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn how to turn shared goals into practical ability by mapping links, explaining standards, and checking what is known.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Map the parts before judging the result
> When a system promises progress, identify what must be aligned before the promise can become useful. Map the partners, funding, standards, routines, and assumptions. Then separate a verified result from a current watch signal.

The World Bank Group reports that Mission 300 has connected more than 50 million people to power across 40 African countries. Its release links the milestone to aligned governments, partners, business support, policy reform, and layered funding. AACSB provides practical guidance and a webinar for teachers working with new standards. An Associated Press headline places a land dispute and resource strain in the current setting, but its article body was not exposed in this run. First Principles Thinking offers a useful method for separating basic facts from inherited assumptions.

Together, these sources support a practical move: treat capacity as a teamwork problem before treating it as a drive problem. Shared systems can widen access when the delivery parts align. Standards become useful when people explain them and connect them to visible practice. A headline can prompt planning without proving a policy or market outcome. Basic questions can expose assumptions worth testing. The sources do not prove equal access, lasting low costs, better learning results, a final world event, or that this method always produces better decisions.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [First Principles Thinking](https://fs.blog/first-principles/)  
> **Useful idea:** Separate what is known from what is assumed, then rebuild the decision from the elements that remain.  
> **Short quote:** “First principles thinking is one of the best ways to discover new solutions.”  
> **Use today:** Before accepting a fixed process or market assumption, list its constraints and ask which ones have evidence.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Map one system from goal to outcome. Identify the partners, standards, assumptions, and evidence needed at each step.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Draw the links behind one operating promise, turn one standard into a visible routine, and test one inherited limit.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to separate a known result, a current watch signal, and a useful method before suggesting action.
