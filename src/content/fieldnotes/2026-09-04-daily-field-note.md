## The Field Note

> [!example] Make the system inspectable
> When a decision, handoff, or result creates friction, do not rely on confidence alone. Show who checks the work, where capacity is unused, how people practice the task, and what evidence supports the diagnosis.

The World Bank Group’s appointment of Yuko Keicho as Vice President and Auditor General describes independent checks, governance, risk management, and internal controls. Stanford GSB highlights an operations question about empty sailing capacity and fleet planning. AACSB lists a workshop on effective case teaching. Hanlon’s Razor offers a useful rule: test neglect, error, or missing facts before assigning bad intent.

Together, these sources support a practical move: build trust into the system. Visible controls help people see how key decisions are checked. Capacity measures reveal losses that confidence can hide. Careful teaching practice makes judgment visible. A fair first diagnosis can keep attention on repair while leaving room to check misconduct when evidence supports it. The sources do not prove better group performance, a specific savings amount, better teaching results, or that every conflict has an innocent cause.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Hanlon’s Razor](https://fs.blog/mental-model-hanlons-razor/)  
> **Useful idea:** Do not assign bad intent when neglect, error, or incomplete information explains the friction.  
> **Short quote:** “Never attribute to malice that which can be adequately explained by neglect.”  
> **Use today:** Before escalating a missed handoff, check the process, information, and ownership gaps first.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Map one consequential decision: identify the evidence, the checking role, the hidden capacity, and the limit of what you can conclude.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Make one control or handoff visible, measure one unused capacity point, and test a process explanation before attributing intent.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Observe how a case discussion is facilitated, then assess whether questions and evidence improve judgment rather than assuming that a workshop or method caused the result.
