## The Field Note

NASA reported on November 24 that all seven DragonCam cameras had been installed, tested and integrated into the Dragonfly rotorcraft by mid-November. Three other instruments still faced delivery, integration and testing. A team could close the camera task while keeping the wider project open.

Think about the update your colleague will inherit on Monday. “Testing complete” sounds reassuring. Add the name of the part, the test it passed and the name of the person waiting for the next result. An installed camera cannot establish that the whole spacecraft is ready to fly.

> [!example] Try this
> Split a rollout into the part you can accept and the next test you still owe.

## Reading Room

**Paper:** Amba et al. (2026), “A qualitative analysis of shift handoff communication practices among tele-ICU clinicians,” Professional Case Management. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/ncm.0000000000000844).

Based on the abstract, clinicians at a remote intensive care center in the US Midwest described differences in what colleagues passed on at shift change. Some wanted a more standard handoff. The interviews also captured barriers and mixed attitudes toward the process. A study at one center cannot establish that a checklist improves outcomes across teams. Try asking both sides of a handoff to write down the next unresolved question. Compare the answers before changing the form. A difference between their answers gives you something specific to discuss.
