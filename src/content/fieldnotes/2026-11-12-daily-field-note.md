> [!quote] Field Note
> Growth needs a capacity test before it needs a bigger promise.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Before expanding a product, program, or partnership, identify the support system that must carry the promise.

## The Field Note

Kauffman describes a shared childcare funding model that joins families, employers, funders, and public agencies. Its shared platform and payment structure aim to reduce paperwork for providers. Kauffman also describes a bioscience group that invested in staff, systems, evaluator training, technology, and work processes after demand exposed a capacity problem. The World Bank makes a similar argument at national scale: a wider economy depends on people, roads and systems, rules, and a capable private sector.

These sources document reports from groups and a national reform plan. They do not prove that a specific expansion plan will succeed or that the same steps transfer unchanged to another setting.

Before approving expansion, name the bottleneck, owner, needed support, and clear measure that would show the system can carry more demand.

> [!example] Try this
> Write four lines before approving scale: the bottleneck, the owner, the required support, and the evidence that would show the system is ready.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Ask what support makes a pathway usable, then separate participation targets from measured outcomes.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Test staffing, quality control, technology, and partner management before accepting more demand.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Name the capability that must grow with a program promise and the evidence that it is ready.

## Reading Room

**Paper:** Shourkaei and Dyck (2025), *Organization*. [Scaling sustainability in businesses with a post-growth orientation](https://doi.org/10.1177/13505084251350847).

Based on the abstract, this early study examined 35 small and medium-sized businesses with a post-growth focus. The authors found four ways to scale social and environmental impact: improvement, expansion, bridging, and teamwork. That gives teams a useful question before adding size: what kind of impact are we trying to scale? The abstract does not show which way works best or prove effects on profits. Its selected businesses also limit transfer to firms pursuing rapid growth. Try comparing a larger operation with a partner that serves the same need. Name the capacity each option requires, then choose one outcome to track.
