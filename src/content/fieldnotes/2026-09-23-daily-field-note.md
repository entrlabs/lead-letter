## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Trace five things before accepting a headline or institutional promise: the claim, the measure, the actor responsible for acting, the capacity required, and the next operational step.

The World Bank describes a leadership pathway connecting research and data to scalable solutions. AACSB argues that communication expertise shapes curriculum and student capability. Inside Higher Ed reports that delayed research funding constrains institutions and researchers. Census provides a current retail-sales measure with a defined scope.

These sources suggest a shared practice question, not one proven cause: who can turn information into a decision, service, or useful next step? The sources do not prove that the appointment will improve development outcomes, that every communication-center restructuring causes harm, that all funding delays share one cause, or that retail sales alone measure business health.

Start with a claim you need to assess this week. Write down what the measure captures and what it leaves out. Name the person or group responsible for acting. Then identify the expertise, funding path, and operating capacity needed to carry the claim into practice.

If that path is unclear, treat the promise as unfinished. A persuasive headline is only the start. The operating choices behind it show whether the evidence can become useful.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Watch What They Do](https://fs.blog/brain-food/september-13-2026/)
> **Useful idea:** Judge a promise by the repeated behavior and operating choices that support it.
> **Use today:** Ask what the institution funds, measures, and repeats, not only what its leaders say.

Farnam Street supplies a thinking prompt, not factual evidence for the cards. Its useful test here is simple: do the institution's repeated choices support the promise it makes?

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Draw the path from a research finding to a usable program. Mark where expertise, funding, or measurement could fail.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Before interpreting a demand or capability claim, name the measure, the decision owner, and the capacity needed to act.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask which expertise improves a service over time, and distinguish visible access from the capability that makes the service better.
