> [!quote] Field Note
> A decision becomes easier to revise when its assumptions are visible before the next consequence arrives.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn how to make trade exposure, crisis practice, maintenance, and uncertainty easier to manage by naming assumptions early.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Make the Assumption Visible Before the Decision Moves
> Before acting on a vivid signal, write down the base rate, the new evidence, the operational exposure, and the trigger that would change your plan.

The Associated Press lists last-minute U.S.-Canada talks aimed at stopping 50% tariffs. That makes cross-border exposure a live operating assumption. AACSB describes a Rowan University class where students rehearse crisis communication through realistic scenarios and role-play. Inside Higher Ed reports deferred maintenance as a long-running institutional and public-policy problem.

Farnam Street's probabilistic-thinking essay supplies the connecting practice. Separate base rates from vivid new events. Update when relevant evidence arrives. Make uncertainty explicit. The sources do not prove that tariffs will end in a particular outcome, that one class produces career success, that one maintenance policy is best, or that probabilistic thinking guarantees better decisions.

They support a narrower move. Write down what you believe, what you are exposed to, and what would make you revise the decision.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> These sources are a current listing headline, teaching case, reported higher-education problem, and practitioner mental-model essay. They do not form one causal test.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [The Value of Probabilistic Thinking: Spies, Crime, and Lightning Strikes](https://fs.blog/probabilistic-thinking/)
> **Useful idea:** Combine prior information with new evidence instead of letting a vivid event replace the base rate.
> **Use today:** For one decision, write the base rate, the new evidence, the exposure it creates, and the observation that would change your mind.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Practice a crisis scenario and state what evidence would change your response.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Map one tariff or supply-chain exposure and define a trigger for revising the plan.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners or leaders to name the base rate, the new evidence, and the first warning sign.
