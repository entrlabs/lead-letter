> Good systems protect human judgment by making capacity, accountability, and learning visible.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise

## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before expanding a program, tool, or organizational change, identify the human capability it depends on, the institution that owns it, and the evidence that would trigger revision.

Today’s sources show four ways capacity becomes visible. HBR reports that workplace norms can make sleep deprivation an organizational condition. Kauffman profiles an aviation program whose pathway depends on exposure, mentorship, fundraising, and durable program capacity. The Federal Reserve shows a formal decision changing the structure of local banking institutions. MIT Sloan reports curriculum changes that connect AI and deep-tech work to governance, judgment, and commercialization.

These sources do not prove one common cause. They support a practical test: when a system expands, ask what human capability it protects, who owns the operating decision, and what evidence would make the plan change.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Map the support system around one opportunity: instruction, mentorship, funding, authority, and the measure that shows whether access became usable.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Name the work norm or governance boundary that protects judgment, then decide what will stop when capacity is full.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Connect emerging tools or career pathways to practice, accountability, and the institution responsible for sustaining them.
