> [!quote] Field Note
> The strongest institutions make the first point of failure visible before they ask people to trust the system.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use one inversion check to make a service, institution, or founder-support system easier to enter and trust.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Make the First Failure Visible
> Before adding a solution, map where a student, founder, partner, or voter could stop trusting the system.

Pew Research Center reports that 55% of U.S. adults were confident the November 2026 midterm elections would be conducted fairly and accurately. That is lower than the comparable 2024 and 2022 measures. The number describes confidence; it does not prove whether the election will be fair or accurate.

AACSB's August 10 case on Kent State's Crawford Hall offers a practical contrast. The authors describe stakeholder input, visible learning spaces, shared facilities, and flexible rooms as ways to make a business school more open to campus and community partners. They report a 5.5% enrollment increase by the building's 2024 opening, but the case does not prove that the building alone caused that result.

The World Bank's jobs FAQ names the less visible side of access: infrastructure, clear rules, private capital, and market connections. A founder can have a good idea and still meet a system that is hard to enter.

The useful move is to invert the problem. Ask what would make the service unwelcoming, the partnership unclear, or the business-support path unusable. Remove one of those conditions before adding another promise.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Inversion and The Power of Avoiding Stupidity](https://fs.blog/inversion/)
> **Useful idea:** Think backward from failure to uncover hidden assumptions and prevent avoidable harm.
> **Short quote:** "Avoiding stupidity is easier than seeking brilliance."
> **Use today:** Write three ways the next initiative could become hard to enter or inspect, then remove one before launch.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> These sources describe different populations and cases. They do not prove one universal cause or remedy.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Which part of a campus process is invisible or difficult to enter before you assume people lack interest?

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> What external constraint could stop a useful idea, service, or partnership from reaching the people it is meant to help?

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Which stakeholder should be invited to name the first point where trust could break?
