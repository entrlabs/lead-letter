> [!quote] Field Note
> A promise becomes usable when the conditions behind it are visible.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn how to test whether a rule, tool, financing promise, or institutional offer is usable in real conditions.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Make the Conditions Visible
> Before acting on a rule, tool, or promise, write the resource condition, judgment owner, observable output, assumption at risk, and response path.

The Associated Press lists current tariff reporting affecting Canadian goods. The World Bank describes SME finance as an ecosystem shaped by policy, institutions, digital infrastructure, and lender capacity. AACSB argues that students need independent reasoning before intensive AI use. Inside Higher Ed lists a conditional tuition-freeze announcement. It shows that an affordability promise depends on timing and conditions.

These examples do not prove firm-level tariff effects, local finance outcomes, causal effects of the AACSB proposal, or implementation of the tuition policy. They support a narrower practice: expose the condition behind a promise before treating it as usable evidence. Farnam Street’s *Surprises* adds a useful question. Which assumption is most likely to fail when the plan meets reality?

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Surprises](https://fs.blog/brain-food/august-23-2026/)
> **Useful idea:** Useful preparation includes recognizing that reality will surprise you.
> **Use today:** Name the assumption most likely to fail when a policy, product, or program meets real conditions.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Show independent reasoning before using AI to accelerate work.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Map the ecosystem condition behind a financing problem and name the assumption that would trigger a course correction.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Publish the funding conditions, review point, and evidence behind an affordability or learning promise.
