> [!quote] Field Note
> A strong label should not make people guess what work is behind it.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Test whether a label, tool, grant, or pilot is backed by visible work.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Make the Work Visible
> Before you use a strong label, write what the label proves, what it does not prove, and what someone should check next.

Some words carry power. Vintage. Coalition. Access. Digital colleague. Readiness. Partnership. Pilot.

Those words can help people move faster. They can also hide weak work.

Today's sources point to the same quiet test. The label has to earn its work.

Harvard Business School described research on vintage products. Consumers did not treat the word as a loose synonym for old or secondhand. They saw vintage as moderately old, iconic, and scarce.

Kauffman described a Kansas City coalition grant only after a planning year. The planning work built relationships, governance, shared measures, and implementation readiness before a three-year, USD 13 million grant moved forward.

The World Bank and SDG 7 custodian agencies showed the same issue at global scale. Energy access is not complete because technology exists. It must reach people who can afford and use it.

MIT Sloan's digital-colleague evidence adds the management version. An AI system does not become a colleague because a team names it that way. Someone still has to own the workflow, the risk, and the decision.

The useful move is simple. When a word gets big, make the work behind it visible.

That does not make every plan succeed. It does make the next test honest.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool: Inversion
> **Mental model:** [Inversion](https://fs.blog/inversion/)  
> **Useful idea:** Work backward from what would make the label fail.  
> **Short quote:** "Avoiding stupidity is easier than seeking brilliance."  
> **Use today:** Ask what would make the name misleading before you ask how to promote it.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> These sources do not prove one universal test for every product, grant, infrastructure project, or AI workflow. They show why strong labels need visible work and clear limits.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Pick one label on your resume or project list. Write one sentence that shows the real work behind it.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Before launching a named program, pilot, or AI workflow, assign an owner and define the evidence it must show.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask students to test big words like impact, innovation, leadership, access, and readiness against visible work.

### Signal Text
