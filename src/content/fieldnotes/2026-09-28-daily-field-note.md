## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before you scale a resource, program, measure, or promise, name who owns the access path. Then name the feedback that would show the system is failing.

The World Bank reports that it mobilized $112 billion in private capital in fiscal year 2026, alongside guarantees, regulatory work, infrastructure, and country strategies. BEA’s current-account release shows why a large number needs component-level reading. Census reports 531,728 business applications in August, but the measure is an early indicator with definition and revision limits. Inside Higher Ed reports that Arizona State University restored the Obama Scholars name one day after changing it, while the program’s actual tuition, housing, and meal support remained the more important access question.

These examples do not establish one common causal rule. They do support one useful practice: do not confuse a resource, number, or label with the opportunity it is meant to create. Ask who can use it, what measure shows that use, and what feedback would make you change the plan.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [The Map Is Not the Territory](https://fs.blog/map-and-territory/)
> **Useful idea:** A measure or policy is a map, not the full situation. Reality is the update.
> **Use today:** Pair every resource, number, or institutional label with one direct check of who can use it and what changes in practice.

Farnam Street supplies a thinking prompt, not factual evidence for the sources above. Its useful test here is simple: check the territory before trusting the map.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Take one headline number or program name and list the access path, the measure, and the claim the source cannot support.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Before adding resources, name the bottleneck and the first observable sign that the new capacity is not reaching the intended user.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask whether a public program label matches the actual access, support, and experience available to students.
