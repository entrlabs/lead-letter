> [!quote] Field Note
> A trusted measure is a map. Growth makes it important to check the territory it leaves out.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Check one trusted measure against direct experience before scaling a product, program, or operating plan.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Try this
> Name the measure you trust, then ask: “What direct experience would show that this measure is incomplete?” Schedule that check before the next scale decision.

Harvard Business Review’s October 30 article reports more recalls among higher-selling vehicles. The research covers millions of sales over a decade. MIT Sloan’s Ferrari case shows a company adding an electric model while protecting its brand identity and controlled growth. The World Bank’s commodity outlook shows how shocks can move through energy, food, fertilizer, and supply. Kauffman’s Guadalupe Centers feature connects economic mobility with the capacity to sustain workforce programs and employer relationships.

These are different cases, not proof of one universal model. They support one practical move. Treat a dashboard, forecast, or plan as a useful map. Then check the people and conditions represented by it. Scale can make a rare defect, hidden dependency, or unsupported promise easier to see.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [The Map Is Not the Territory](https://fs.blog/map-and-territory/)
> **Useful idea:** Models simplify reality. They need feedback because the territory can change faster than the map.
> **Use today:** Pair one important measure with one direct observation from the people, customers, or conditions it represents.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> What part of your project is missing from the measure you currently trust?

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Which customer signal, defect, or exception should be reviewed before the next growth decision?

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to separate what a model shows from what it cannot show.
