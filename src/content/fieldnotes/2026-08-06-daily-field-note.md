> [!quote] Field Note
> The useful signal is not the loudest claim. It is the one that includes what would prove it wrong.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn to turn a daily signal into a clear test that can change your decision.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before you act on a claim, write the observation that would make you revise your stance.

Schools, employers, and policymakers still need shared definitions of skill and proof.
Jobs programs still rely on many moving conditions, not a single policy lever.
Engagement still depends on everyday manager routines.
AI in business education still works best when it sharpens judgment, not speed.

The same pattern repeats: signals are useful when they are falsifiable.

Choose one decision to test this week.
If the evidence appears and the claim still holds, keep it.
If not, change course quickly.

That single discipline protects students, teams, and institutions from acting on confirmation-only signals.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Falsification: How to Destroy Incorrect Ideas](https://fs.blog/peter-cathcart-wason-falsification/)  
> **Useful idea:** Run one decision through a condition that could prove it wrong, then act only if it still works.  
> **Short quote:** "The only way to win the game is to guess strings of numbers that would prove your beloved hypothesis wrong."  
> **Use today:** Pick one signal and define one observation that would change your next move.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> What evidence would make someone revise one skill claim you currently treat as settled?

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Which assumption in your next operating decision would change if one data point went the other way?

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> What student response would make you change how you assess that decision?
