> [!quote] Field Note
> Before you scale a plan, test the bottleneck that could make agreement, access, teaching, or local enterprise fail.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use one disconfirming question to expose the constraint most likely to break a plan before rollout.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Test Before You Scale
> Name the likely constraint, the observation that would disprove your plan, and the person who owns the test.

A change plan can look aligned because disagreement stayed vague or unsafe. A shipping network can appear competitive until congestion reveals who controls scarce terminal capacity. A teaching requirement can exist on paper while educators lack materials, practice, or feedback. A local-enterprise program can identify demand without building the capabilities needed to serve it.

Each case points to a different bottleneck. The shared mistake is scaling before testing the constraint.

Use a three-part pre-rollout check:

1. **Constraint:** What scarce agreement, access, skill, or capability is the plan assuming?
2. **Disconfirmation:** What observation would show that assumption is wrong?
3. **Owner:** Who will run the test, report the result, and adjust the plan?

Ask for evidence that could change the decision, not only evidence that makes the preferred plan look stronger. A small disconfirming test can surface false alignment, unavailable capacity, weak implementation support, or a missing local capability while the plan is still inexpensive to revise.

The sources do not show that one bottleneck explains every failure. They support a disciplined sequence: test the most consequential assumption, then scale what survives.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool: Falsification
> **Mental model:** [Falsification](https://fs.blog/peter-cathcart-wason-falsification/)  
> **Useful idea:** Look for evidence that could prove the preferred plan wrong, not only evidence that confirms it.  
> **Short quote:** “It feels better to find evidence that confirms what you believe.”  
> **Use today:** Ask each decision owner for one observation that would disconfirm the plan.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> One bottleneck or one test does not explain every implementation failure.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> State the finding that would make you change your recommendation, then look for it before presenting your answer.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Name the agreement or capacity bottleneck most likely to break the rollout, assign its test, and set the decision that follows each result.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Pair every new requirement with the materials, practice, feedback, and ownership needed to make implementation possible.
