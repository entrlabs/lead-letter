> [!quote] Field Note
> Measure the system before you change the system.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use a visible exposure map, data handoff, and category audit before committing to a solution.

## The Field Note

Good decisions begin with a usable picture of the system. NASA’s California mapping work makes wildfire exposure visible at one-square-meter resolution. Terra’s retirement shows why a valuable data record needs a transition plan even when the platform is aging. Lindacher’s review adds an organizational warning: categories and communication routines can turn complexity into exclusion.

The lesson is practical. Before approving a new program, vendor, or resilience measure, identify what is being measured, who owns the handoff, and which category could hide a relevant case. That will not guarantee a good outcome. It makes the next decision easier to inspect.

> [!example] Try this
> For one current decision, write the measurement, owner, handoff, and excluded case on one page.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Before choosing a project or research path, name the evidence that would change your mind and the date you will review it.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Map one operational exposure, its owner, the handoff, and the case your current categories could miss.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Audit one intake or advising process for categories that simplify complexity by excluding students who do not fit the default path.

## Reading Room

**Paper:** Tanja Lindacher (2026), “Inclusive” education as a catalyst for organizational change? A systems-theoretical analysis of the logic of organizational decision-making in the education system, *Journal of Organizational Change Management*, 39(6), 1197-1220. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/JOCM-01-2026-0071).

The abstract presents a systems-theoretical analysis of how organizations process or resist inclusive education through decision programming. This card is based on the abstract. It identifies operational closure mechanisms that translate socioeconomic complexity into meritocratic distinctions and communication aspects that exclude parts of the student body. The paper is a conceptual review, so it does not test a specific intervention or establish a causal effect. Use it as a prompt to inspect the categories and communication routines through which an institution decides who receives attention. **Read depth:** abstract only; publisher landing page was blocked by a Cloudflare verification screen.
