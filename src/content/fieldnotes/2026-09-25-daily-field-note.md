## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before acting on an opportunity headline, ask four questions: What barrier stands between the offer and the person who needs it? Who is responsible for removing that barrier? What measure will show whether access improved? What time horizon makes the claim meaningful?

Harvard Business School reports a long-run shift away from locally rooted, high-capital self-employment. Kauffman describes grants that connect hiring practices, childcare, transportation, and digital access. AACSB summarizes uneven workplace AI adoption and the extra implementation work it can create. Census provides the market context of 531,728 August business applications, down 7.8% from July.

These sources document different measures and scopes. They do not prove that the grants will improve outcomes, that uneven AI use caused an employment difference, or that applications predict venture survival. They support a disciplined practice: name the barrier, actor, measure, and time horizon before treating an opportunity as usable.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Slop](https://fs.blog/brain-food/september-20-2026/)
> **Useful idea:** A shortcut that saves one person time can create hidden cleanup work for someone else.
> **Use today:** When assessing a new hiring, AI, or service process, ask who receives the extra checking, repair, and coordination work.

Farnam Street supplies a thinking prompt, not factual evidence for the cards. Its useful test here is simple: who receives the work that a new process creates?

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Match each source to its measure, actor, and time horizon. Mark the point where an interpretation would exceed the evidence.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Before acting on an opportunity claim, name the practical barrier, the owner of the next step, and the measure that will test access.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to compare business applications, employment patterns, planned interventions, and AI adoption without treating them as interchangeable outcomes.
