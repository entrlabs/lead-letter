> [!quote] Field Note
> When the number changes, can you still see the number that shaped the decision?

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Save the input, rule and assumption behind a decision before the dashboard or policy page changes.

## The Field Note

Suppose a university team forecasts its incoming class from a model and a published enrollment rule. Later, the school learns that its count crossed the permitted share of nonresident students. The final cost is not only a result. It is also a record of which students counted, which rule applied and which estimate the team used.

The same problem appears in other settings. The United Nations described attacks on Ukraine’s energy grid and Black Sea shipping as risks that reach homes, businesses and food systems. CISA added two exploited vulnerabilities to its catalog with November 26 due dates. A headline gives you a reason to look. It cannot tell you whether your own route, product or system is exposed.

Save the first number beside the decision. Save the rule version beside the calculation. When the source changes, review the original call and the new evidence separately.

> [!example] Try this
> At your next review, put the first input, the current input and the rule version in three adjacent columns.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Put the release date beside every number in your chart. Which conclusion depends on a later revision?

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Attach the rule version and original input to one operating decision. Could a colleague reproduce your calculation?

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Give learners the first and revised values. Ask them to judge the original choice before seeing the revision.

## Reading Room

**Paper:** Jingjing Tang, Aaron Rumack, Bryan Wilder, and Roni Rosenfeld (2025), “Real-time Forecasting of Data Revisions in Epidemic Surveillance Streams.” [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013709).

Based on the abstract, the paper describes Delphi Revision Forecast, a method that uses earlier revisions to forecast later finalized surveillance values. Its abstract reports useful accuracy across several disease-reporting streams and more than 10-100x computational efficiency compared with existing methods. The study is about public-health surveillance, so it does not prove that the method transfers unchanged to business or education data. Try keeping the first dashboard value beside the later value and label the revision before judging the original decision. **Read depth:** abstract and metadata; full text not retrieved.
