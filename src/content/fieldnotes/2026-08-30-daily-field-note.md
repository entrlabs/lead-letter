> A promise becomes usable when its cost, control owner, support path, and evidence-to-use route are visible.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> See how governance, finance, legal exposure, and research pathways shape whether an institutional promise can be used.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Make the Cost and Control Path Visible
> For one active initiative, name the responsibility owner, cost or risk carrier, support needed for adoption, evidence-to-use pathway, and update trigger.

The World Bank Group describes an independent assurance role. It covers governance, accountability, risk management, and internal controls. The World Bank’s SME Finance page treats access as a broad system. It includes policy, institutions, digital tools, and financial products. Inside Higher Ed reports that Saint Augustine’s University is working through bankruptcy and legal-cost questions while it is not offering classes this fall. AACSB’s research-impact framework describes pathways through teaching, practice, policy, and society rather than treating publication as the endpoint.

These cases differ, but they support one bounded practice: make the carrying system visible before treating a goal as complete. The sources do not prove improved performance, universal financing or adoption, or institutional survival. They also do not prove greater research impact or better decisions from using the mental model.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Separate a stated promise from the cost, mechanism, and evidence pathway that could make delivery visible.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
