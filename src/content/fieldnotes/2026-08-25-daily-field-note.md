> [!quote] Field Note
> A useful system explains not only what it measures, but what its measurement makes people do.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use a simple second-order test to judge whether a metric, tool, or promise will help people in real conditions.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Ask What Happens Next
> Before acting on a metric, tool, or promise, write its immediate result, next likely response, affected person, judgment owner, and review path.

The Associated Press lists current reporting on workplace surveillance and on workers in China adapting to pressure from AI at work. The World Bank describes SME finance as an ecosystem shaped by policy, institutions, digital infrastructure, and lender capacity. AACSB's current Insights page promotes a 2026 Framework for Research Impact.

These examples do not prove the effects or legality of surveillance, the scale of AI-related job change, local finance outcomes, or research adoption. They support a narrower practice: inspect the behavior a system creates, not only the result it promises. Farnam Street's *Second-Order Thinking* adds a direct question: what happens next?

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Second-Order Thinking](https://fs.blog/second-order-thinking/)
> **Useful idea:** A first result can create a second response that changes the real outcome.
> **Use today:** Name the next two likely responses before adopting a metric, policy, or tool.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Ask what a tool helps you skip and what judgment you still need to show.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Publish the purpose, limits, affected people, and challenge path for a metric or monitoring system.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Define the audience, decision, and pathway behind a research-impact or learning promise.
