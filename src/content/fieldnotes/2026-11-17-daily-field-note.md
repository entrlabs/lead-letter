## The Field Note

Good decisions are easier to improve when their assumptions are visible. A vendor relationship, a continuity plan, or a program change can become difficult to revise when no one has named the failure mode or the next review point.

The Federal Reserve, FDIC, and OCC recently extended the comment period for proposed third-party risk guidance. The notice describes a principles-based approach that can be tailored to the relationship. That is a useful prompt for separating current requirements from provisional safeguards.

NASA’s Voyager 1 update offers a different example. The mission continues operating more than 16 billion miles from Earth. Distance and delayed feedback make continuity planning unavoidable. The analogy is useful, but it does not prove that a business system will behave like a spacecraft.

Spotlight PA’s report on Albright College adds a human consequence. When programs change, current students need individual plans, not only a portfolio announcement.

> [!example] Try this
> Before approving one important change, write the assumption, owner, failure mode, and next review date on one page.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Before choosing a project path, write down what would change your mind and when you will check it.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> For one vendor or system, name the failure mode, monitoring owner, exit option, and next review date.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Pair every program or pathway change with an individual plan and a clear next decision point.

## Reading Room

**Paper:** Sarah Wieland and Audrey Amrein-Beardsley (2025), “A New Transfer Advising Model to Promote Increased Student Transfer Success: A Mixed-Methods, Action Research Study,” *The Mentor: Innovative Scholarship on Academic Advising*, 27, 26–69. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.59236/mj2763333).

The abstract reports a mixed-methods action research study across two community colleges and one university. Students who worked with the same adviser across institutions were reported to persist more successfully through transfer and university enrollment, with higher advising satisfaction and transfer student capital than the comparison group. This account is based on the abstract only. It does not establish how the model performs in other settings or with different staffing. Use it as a prompt to test whether continuity of advising could reduce avoidable handoff loss in your own transition process. **Read depth:** abstract only.
