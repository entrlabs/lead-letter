> [!quote] Field Note
> A trusted promise has a visible control path behind it.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use a short visibility test to judge whether a policy, process, or institution can be trusted in practice.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Make the Control Path Visible
> Before relying on a policy, process, or institution, write its purpose, control owner, missing information, observable output, and review trigger.

The World Bank announced an independent Auditor General remit covering assurance, governance, risk, and controls. AACSB's current article argues that accreditation needs a purpose people can understand, not only compliance activity. Inside Higher Ed lists Saint Augustine's University as unable to offer fall classes after bankruptcy. The Associated Press lists current tariff reporting affecting Canadian goods.

These examples do not prove later audit performance, causal accreditation effects, the full bankruptcy outcome, or firm-level tariff effects. They support a narrower practice: make the control path visible before treating a promise or summary as usable evidence. Farnam Street's *All Models Are Wrong* adds a useful limit: a model helps only within the situation it can represent.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [All Models Are Wrong](https://fs.blog/all-models-are-wrong/)
> **Useful idea:** A model is useful only when its limits fit the decision.
> **Use today:** Name what the summary leaves out, who checks it, and what observable result would trigger review.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Ask what an institutional promise means for continuity and who can verify it.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Map the control owner and missing operating condition behind a plan.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Connect accreditation and financial warnings to a visible student-support path.
