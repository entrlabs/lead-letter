## The Field Note

A student can meet a college deadline and still lack the money, transport, or information needed to arrive. Waiting for a missed deadline can make that problem harder to solve.

In a November 13 account, Kauffman describes Youth Lead KC's work with students in Kansas City. A summer intake process helped staff identify financial and practical barriers before the next stage of college. The group paired staff follow-up and data systems with trusted relationships.

This is a funder's account of an organization it supports. It does not prove that the process raised graduation rates. A form completed and a barrier resolved are different results.

Choose one point where students can ask for help. Check who receives the request, when they reply, and whether the student can reach the next step. If reports sit unanswered, another form adds little.

> [!example] Try this
> Review the first point at which a student can report a practical barrier. Give that report an owner and a response date before a missed deadline becomes the first warning.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Name one barrier that could interrupt your next step and identify the person who can help resolve it.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> For a service you run, trace one request from intake to a named owner and a completed response.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask a small group of students where they would seek help with a financial or administrative problem, then compare their answers with your intended route.

## Reading Room

**Paper:** Ruth Geraldine Melonie Hoskins and Sharmla Rama (2026), Embedding equity-centred, proactive academic advising in first-year orientation: A case study of the College of Humanities, University of KwaZulu-Natal, Journal of Student Affairs in Africa, 14(1). [Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.24085/jsaa.v14i1.7127).

Hoskins and Rama examined student feedback on academic advising embedded in first-year orientation. Of about 1,000 participants, 531 responded. Students reported stronger feelings of capability, connection, purpose, and resourcefulness, while digital literacy, connectivity, and rollout timing limited participation. This account is based on the abstract. This single university case does not establish that advising caused better retention, and the respondents may differ from students who did not respond. The useful application is to test whether students can reach support before judging the support itself. Ask who missed the intervention, what blocked access, and whether a different channel would reach them.
