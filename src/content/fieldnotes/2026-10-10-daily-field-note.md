> [!quote] Field Note
> A decision becomes more trustworthy when ownership, cost, and review evidence are visible.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn to name who controls a decision, who carries its costs, and what evidence should trigger a change.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before scaling a tool, program, or channel, name the decision owner, the exposed asset or relationship, the burden measure, and the review point.

Four sources ask us to look at who owns a decision and who bears its cost. HBR asks how AI agents may change the link between a store and its customers. The World Bank uses Maya Bay to show why a place needs people and rules to care for it. Federal Reserve data pair household gains with signs of financial strain. AACSB describes a class where teachers and students work through uncertain problems together.

These sources do not prove one common cause or one solution. They suggest a useful check: name the owner, what is at risk, who bears the cost, and what would trigger review.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Map one decision path. Name who controls it, who is affected, what evidence is visible, and what remains hidden.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Choose one customer or team decision. Name its owner, the asset or relationship at risk, the burden measure, and the review point.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Make one applied-learning decision visible by identifying who carries the uncertainty, what support is available, and what evidence would change the design.
