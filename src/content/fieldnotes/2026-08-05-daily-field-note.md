> [!quote] Field Note
> A promising claim becomes useful when you can name the evidence that would change your mind.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn to turn a promising signal into a test that can change your mind.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Test the Claim
> Before you trust a signal, write the observation that would make you revise it.

Skills-first labour markets need shared definitions, recognition, and portable proof. Jobs strategies need firms, infrastructure, finance, and rules that can sustain hiring. Engagement data needs manager-level changes, not only a larger dashboard. AI learning needs students to question and evaluate outputs, not only generate them.

These source lanes are different. The useful pattern is the test behind the claim.

Ask one question before you act: what would show that my interpretation is wrong?

That question changes the work. A student can test whether a claimed skill is visible in a real artifact. A manager can test whether a changed routine improves clarity or support. A policymaker can test whether a jobs program changes firm conditions. An educator can test whether AI use makes judgment more visible.

The sources do not prove that one model works everywhere. They do support a better habit: do not collect only confirming evidence. Name the observation that would make you revise the claim, then go look for it.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool: Falsification
> **Mental model:** [Falsification: How to Destroy Incorrect Ideas](https://fs.blog/peter-cathcart-wason-falsification/)  
> **Useful idea:** Test a claim with an example that could prove it wrong, not only one that confirms it.  
> **Short quote:** "The only way to win the game is to guess strings of numbers that would prove your beloved hypothesis wrong."  
> **Use today:** Write one disconfirming observation beside the next signal you plan to trust.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> The source set combines policy analysis, institutional pages, survey reporting, and a practitioner mental model. It does not establish causal effects for every setting.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> What evidence would make someone revise their view of one skill you claim to have?

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> What observation would show that your next operating assumption is wrong?

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> What student response would make you change the way you teach or assess this claim?
