> Better tools do not remove the need for better questions, readiness, and judgment.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Find the human or system constraint that could defeat a strategy before scaling a tool, promise, or programme.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Test the Bottleneck Before the Tool
> Before adding a tool or promise, name the human or system condition that could make the plan fail.

The Associated Press reports that retail sales slumped after a summer tax-refund boost faded. That is a reminder to separate durable customer demand from temporary support before expanding capacity.

Harvard Business Review makes the same move inside organizations. Assess whether a team is ready to execute a strategy before rolling it out. Its research review on generative AI adds a warning. Faster ideation or screening can deepen human bottlenecks such as fixation, bias, and overconfidence in polished output.

The Kauffman Foundation's announcement of six Kansas City nonprofit leaders as award finalists points to a related condition. Community work depends on local people who carry trust and practical knowledge. MIT Sloan's analysis of AI and climate adds a system warning. A local efficiency gain can create new demand and rebound effects.

These sources do not prove that one readiness check, AI policy, recognition programme, or market interpretation causes better outcomes everywhere. They do support a practical move: write the bottleneck, the missing capability or evidence, and one early warning sign before scaling.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Inversion and The Power of Avoiding Stupidity](https://fs.blog/inversion/)
> **Useful idea:** Think backward about what would discourage the desired result or create harm, then remove or test those conditions.
> **Short quote:** "Spend less time trying to be brilliant and more time trying to avoid obvious stupidity."
> **Use today:** Write three ways a plan could fail before adding another action.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> These sources support a practical readiness check, not one causal test of a single plan.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Ask what assumption would make a project or programme fail before judging its promise.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Check team readiness, customer demand, and the way a new tool could reinforce the bottleneck.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners or leaders to name the missing condition, the evidence needed, and the first warning sign.
