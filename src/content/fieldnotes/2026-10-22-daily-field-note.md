> [!quote] Field Note
> Scale is easier to trust when the work, the people, the support, and the review point are visible.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use four current examples to test whether a promise has visible work, responsible people, support, review, and evidence.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before scaling a promise, map the work, name the decision owner, listen to the people affected, and define the evidence that would earn the next step.

MIT Sloan's October 20 article on systematic innovation with AI recommends breaking work into smaller tasks, mapping existing workflows, keeping humans responsible for decisions, and adding review steps. The Kauffman Foundation's account of Ewing Kauffman's leadership describes a related practice: know people beyond their job titles and make room for their contribution. It also shows that a promise may need more support than funding alone.

The same test applies to institutions. The World Bank's announcement that the UAE will host the 2029 Annual Meetings creates a platform for global dialogue, but the platform itself is not an outcome. AACSB's survey of business-school leaders shows why the work is difficult: technology, finances, learner expectations, employer expectations, fundraising, and scrutiny arrive together.

The useful question is not whether a plan sounds ambitious. It is whether a person can see the work, exercise the needed authority, find the support, and show what happened at the review point.

These sources include expert guidance, a foundation retrospective, an official press release, and an association survey. They support a practice for inspection and review. They do not prove universal causal results or outcomes from future events.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> In a group project, draw the work from promise to result. Mark who decides, who needs support, and what evidence would show progress.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Before automating or expanding one process, ask the people closest to it to show the current steps, the first failure point, and the review decision.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> When an institution announces a new opportunity, ask what support, participation path, owner, and outcome evidence will make the promise usable.
