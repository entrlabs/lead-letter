> [!quote] Field Note
> A promise becomes useful when the person responsible can name the constraint that still blocks action.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Choose one important task or initiative, name the condition blocking action, and design a small test that removes it.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Try this
> Before handing off an important task, name the decision boundary, the support available, and the condition that could stop progress.

Delegation is not complete when a task leaves your list. It is complete when another person has enough authority, information, and support to act.

Harvard Business Review's October 22 collection presents delegation as a management practice that can empower employees. The guidance is practical, not a controlled evaluation of every tip. It does not prove that one delegation method works in every team.

The same constraint appears in a different setting. Kauffman's account of Red Tail Academy describes a youth aviation pathway that needed fundraising capacity and planned revenue streams to support its growth. An inviting opportunity still needs the capacity to deliver it.

For one initiative, write four lines: the action, the bottleneck, the authority or support required, and the smallest test that could show progress. Give the responsible person a clear boundary for deciding and a date for review.

If the test fails, do not treat the failure as proof that the idea is weak. Ask whether the constraint was named correctly, whether the person had usable authority, or whether the test measured the intended change. Revise one condition before adding more scale.

> **Use today:** Name the goal and the one constraint that must not be traded away.

This is a practitioner thinking prompt, not evidence that the practice works in every setting.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Choose one group-project task. Name who can decide, what support they need, and what evidence would show the handoff is working.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Delegate one outcome with a decision boundary, a support commitment, and a review date. Do not transfer responsibility without usable authority.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to separate demand for a pathway from the capacity required to deliver it, then design a small capacity test.
