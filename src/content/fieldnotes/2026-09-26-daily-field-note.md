> [!quote] Field Note

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn to name the resource, decision-maker, hidden burden, and safety check behind a claim about capacity.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before adding capacity or autonomy, ask four questions: What resource is moving? Who has authority to decide? Who carries the hidden checking or cleanup work? What safety check or threshold triggers intervention?

The World Bank reports record private-capital mobilization alongside guarantees, regulatory changes, and coordination. Harvard Business Review advises overloaded leaders to make trade-offs explicit and delegate authority with responsibility. Inside Higher Ed reports a proposed grant framework that could widen termination discretion and create planning uncertainty. Stanford GSB summarizes research on AI systems that defer at risky moments and on calibrated human oversight.

These sources document different settings and evidence types. They do not prove that mobilized capital creates a particular number of jobs, that workload practices reduce attrition, that the proposed grant rule will become final, or that research oversight methods are safe in live organizations. They support a practical discipline: make the operating burden and the accountable check visible before calling a system scalable or trustworthy.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Slop](https://fs.blog/brain-food/september-20-2026/)
> **Useful idea:** A shortcut that saves one person time can shift checking and cleanup work to someone else.
> **Use today:** When reviewing a grant process, workload plan, financing model, or AI system, name who carries the hidden verification cost.

Farnam Street supplies a thinking prompt, not factual evidence for the cards. Its useful test here is simple: who receives the work that a new process creates?

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Map each source to its resource, decision-maker, hidden burden, and safety check. Mark where the interpretation exceeds what the source establishes.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Before adding a priority or automating a decision, show what will stop, who owns the call, who checks the result, and what threshold triggers a pause.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to compare funding, workload, grant policy, and AI oversight without treating mobilized capital, advice, proposed rules, and research models as the same kind of outcome.
