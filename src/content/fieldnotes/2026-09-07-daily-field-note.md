> A promising idea survives contact with reality only when the system around it can carry the work.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn how to inspect coordination, finance, demand, and sustainable effort before scaling a promising idea.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Scale the system, not only the idea
> Before scaling a promise, map the conditions that must hold. Identify who must coordinate and what financing enables delivery. Mark uncertain demand assumptions. Check where sustained effort could become unsustainable.

The World Bank Group describes Mission 300 as coordinated work across public, private, and philanthropic partners. Its release reports more than 50 million electricity connections across 40 African countries, alongside major financing commitments. The report supports a coordination lesson. It does not prove that the full target will be met or that access will be equal.

The same question appears at a smaller scale. The World Bank places SME finance inside an enabling environment that includes policy, institutions, digital infrastructure, and private capital. AP's current Business index exposes a headline about tourism groups trying to win Canadian visitors back during a strained trade relationship, but the article body was not accessible in this run. AACSB's current resources point toward research impact and deliberate case teaching. Farnam Street warns that discipline can become costly when it is used to push through months rather than difficult days.

Together, these sources support a practical move: treat capacity as part of the idea. Map the dependencies and test the financing assumption. Separate a headline from measured demand evidence. Redesign the workload before asking people to push harder. These sources do not prove that coordination guarantees delivery or that finance interventions produce equal growth. They do not establish a measured tourism decline or improved AACSB outcomes. The practitioner warning is not a clinical finding.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [The Map Is Not the Territory](https://fs.blog/map-and-territory/)
> **Useful idea:** A plan or dashboard represents reality, but it is not reality.
> **Short quote:** “The map is not the territory.”
> **Use today:** Compare one operating map with an observed customer, student, supplier, or community experience before scaling a decision.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Choose one promising idea and map its dependencies. Label each condition as observed, reported, uncertain, or assumed.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Test the financing and demand assumptions behind one growth plan, then inspect whether the current workload can sustain delivery.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to compare a model of success with one observed case and identify which missing condition could change the recommendation.
