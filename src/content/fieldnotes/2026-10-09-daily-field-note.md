## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Choose one result that matters. Name the process behind it, assign responsibility for that process, and identify the evidence that would show the process is weakening.

Today’s sources show four ways the work behind a result can be missed. An HBR analysis uses imitation to reveal where competitive distinction may sit. AACSB describes connected courses that need deliberate design to build student capabilities. The World Bank’s Maya Bay example shows that a natural resource depends on the people and institutions that maintain it. A Federal Reserve enforcement release shows how a formal control can fail in implementation.

These sources do not prove one common causal mechanism. They do support a bounded practice: inspect the process behind a visible result, name who owns it, and define evidence that would reveal a gap.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Take a finished result and identify the process it reveals, the process it hides, and the evidence that would test your interpretation.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Choose one customer or team result. Name the delivery process, its owner, and the early sign that would trigger a review.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Trace one learning or program outcome to the connections, support, and accountability that make it possible.
