> [!quote] Field Note
> A promise becomes durable when its capacity, partners, users, and evidence are visible.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use four current examples to identify the capabilities, partners, and evidence required before expansion.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before expanding an idea, name the essential work, accountable owner, support gap, partner role, and evidence that would change the plan.

The World Bank Group's new Philanthropy Hub links capital, knowledge, and partners. Kauffman's Red Tail Academy account shows a founder-led program adding fundraising and revenue capacity as it grows.

AACSB's research-impact framework asks business schools to look beyond publication and citation counts. It points toward use across practice, policy, education, and society. MIT Sloan's management analysis says firms create value from widely available AI through trust, data, expertise, governance, and redesigned work.

These are different institutional examples, not proof of one scaling formula. They support a practical test: name what must be built around the promise before the promise is expanded. Make the decision owner, support system, partner network, and revision evidence explicit. The sources describe distinct initiatives and frameworks; they do not prove that any one approach will produce a specific outcome.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Map one program's promise, essential work, decision owner, partner roles, and evidence that someone actually used it.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Before expanding, identify the capability or control your team lacks and name the smallest test that could expose the gap.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Define who should use a project, what support makes use possible, and what evidence would show value beyond completion or citation.
