> [!quote] Field Note
> The first result is only the start of the decision; the system it creates is the real test.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use a second-order check to see whether a policy, program, or founder decision improves the system after launch.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Check What Follows the First Result
> Before treating a result as success, map the next two effects and the people who will feel them.

AACSB's 2026 research-impact framework asks business schools to look beyond publication and citation counts toward contributions to business, policy, education, and society. The report describes impact as nonlinear and iterative, with pathways through scholarship, teaching, and external engagement.

The World Bank's jobs FAQ names the surrounding conditions that shape whether firms can grow and hire: infrastructure, clear rules, capital, and access to markets. It also warns that an apparent skills shortage may instead be a problem of information, location, wages, working conditions, discrimination, or informality.

The IMF's public topic structure places governance, fiscal policy, artificial intelligence, and digital finance in the same wider policy field. That does not prove a single cause, but it is a useful reminder that technical choices can create institutional consequences.

The practical move is second-order thinking. Ask what happens next, who responds, and what could become worse even if the first result looks good. Then test one of those consequences before scaling the decision.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Second-Order Thinking: What Smart People Use to Outperform](https://fs.blog/second-order-thinking/)
> **Useful idea:** Ask "And then what?" to examine interactions, time, and consequences beyond the immediate result.
> **Short quote:** "First-order thinking is fast and easy."
> **Use today:** Write the likely effects in 10 minutes, 10 months, and 10 years before launch.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> These sources are frameworks and guidance, not one causal test of a single intervention.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Ask what a policy or program changes after its first visible result.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Map how customers, suppliers, employees, and regulators may respond next.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Measure stakeholder use and pathway evidence alongside output counts.
