## The Field Note

The National Weather Service briefing page shows national weather hazards and links to forecasts for specific places. A colored area on the national map can tell a manager where to look. It cannot tell a delivery driver whether a particular bridge is open.

Start with the trip in question. Write down where it begins, which stretch may be exposed and when the driver expects to pass. Then check the local forecast and the road authority. Someone on the route may have newer information than the person looking at the map.

> [!example] Try this
> Give the shift lead a time to revisit a weather call before staff set out.

## Reading Room

**Paper:** Iqbal et al. (2025), “Benevolent leadership enhances organizational learning through communication, trust, and knowledge sharing,” Scientific Reports. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-15170-x).

Based on the abstract, Iqbal and colleagues surveyed 350 people in organizations where staff depend on specialist knowledge. They found that trust and open communication mattered to the link between supportive leadership and learning. A survey cannot establish which came first. Use the finding to frame a question for a shift meeting. When a map and a driver’s report disagree, can the driver say so before the schedule is set? Ask for one example of someone raising a concern and what the team did with it.
