> [!quote] Field Note
> Trust becomes testable when the terms behind a promise are visible.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn how to test whether a promise makes its monitoring, resources, measures, identity, and response conditions clear.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Make the Terms of Trust Visible
> Before asking for trust or cooperation, write the monitored input, resource condition, outcome measure, accountable owner, identity risk, and expected response.

The Associated Press shows workplace surveillance as an active management issue. The World Bank describes SME finance as an ecosystem. It depends on policy, institutions, digital infrastructure, and lender capacity. AACSB’s research-impact framework makes societal value part of how business education describes its work. Inside Higher Ed reports a live university branding dispute. The report shows that names can carry institutional value and legal risk.

These examples do not prove that surveillance has a particular effect. They do not prove that a local finance program will succeed, that research metrics cause impact, or that the branding case will end a particular way. They support a narrower practice: make the terms of trust visible before treating a promise as evidence. Farnam Street’s Reciprocity model adds a useful question. What response does this action make more likely?

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Reciprocity: Getting What You Give](https://fs.blog/reciprocity/)
> **Useful idea:** Actions shape the responses that follow, so the conditions of an exchange matter.
> **Use today:** State the condition, measure, owner, and expected response before asking people to cooperate.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Ask what evidence would show that an institution’s promise is working.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Map the ecosystem condition behind a financing or growth problem, then name what your monitoring system can and cannot prove.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Treat research measures, institutional names, and public promises as governance choices whose terms should be made legible.
