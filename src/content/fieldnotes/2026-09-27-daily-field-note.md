> [!NOTE]
> Before you scale a result, name the enabling system, the measured constraint, and the evidence that would make you revise the claim.

> [!quote] Field Note
> Scale is not only the size of the result. It is the strength of the system that lets people use the result.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn to identify the enabling system, measured constraint, and revision risk behind a claim about growth or capacity.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before you scale a program, market, campus investment, or operating change, name the condition that makes the result usable. Then name the measure that could prove the condition is missing.

The World Bank describes Mission 300 as a coordinated effort that connected more than 50 million people to electricity across 40 African countries. The effort combines financing, policy reform, country plans, and private participation. BEA's second-quarter data show why scale also needs measurement: the U.S. current-account deficit widened to $246.0 billion, but the component flows moved in different directions. Census labels its $773.9 billion August retail and food-services figure an advance estimate. Inside Higher Ed reports a $1 billion gift for an engineering college, but the gift's eventual educational effect will depend on institutional design.

These examples do not establish one common causal rule. They do support one useful practice: when a result looks large, inspect the system around it. Ask who owns the enabling work, which constraint is being measured, and what evidence would make you revise the claim.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Inversion and The Power of Avoiding Stupidity](https://fs.blog/inversion/)
> **Useful idea:** Ask what would make the desired outcome fail before adding a new intervention.
> **Use today:** Before scaling, list the missing infrastructure, unclear decision rights, weak measure, or revision risk that could make the result unusable.

Farnam Street supplies a thinking prompt, not factual evidence for the sources above. Its useful test here is simple: identify the failure condition before adding resources.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Take one headline about growth and identify the enabling system, the measure, and the claim the source cannot support.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Before adding resources, name the bottleneck and the first observable sign that the new capacity is not reaching the intended user.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask what a large investment changes for access, learning, research, and governance, then separate the promise from the evidence.

## Useful Copy

### Signal Text

> [!abstract] Social Copy
> A large result is not the same as durable capacity. Before you scale, name the system that makes the result usable, the constraint you can measure, and the evidence that would make you change course.
