> Better work starts when people can see how a decision, handoff, or lesson actually happens.

> Use three questions to find where a team, process, or learning plan is breaking down.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Make the Work Visible
> Ask the people doing the work, map the steps they take, and check who has the power to change them.

When a project stalls, people often say the team needs to work better together. That is too vague to fix. The useful question is simpler: where does the work stop making sense to the people who must carry it out?

Start with the people closest to the work. Harvard Business Review reports that leaders often see themselves as good collaborators while the people they work with see something different. That gap does not prove that every manager is unaware. It does show why feedback from others is more useful than a leader's own impression.

Then map the process before adding a new tool. Another Harvard Business Review article warns that companies can automate an old process without redesigning it. The article cites surveys on limited enterprise-wide returns from AI. Those surveys do not predict any one firm's results. They do support a sensible pause: identify the needless step before speeding it up.

The same idea matters in education. Stanford Social Innovation Review describes a Wesleyan program that teaches students to study systems and power, not only to pitch startups. It is one program's account, not proof that every course should use its model. Its useful prompt is clear: help learners see who can approve, block, or reshape a solution.

Use these three questions today: What do the people doing the work say? Which step creates the delay or confusion? Who has the authority to change that step? The answers turn a broad complaint into a problem a team can test.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Choose one group project. Write the decision, the next step, and the person who can approve it. Ask what remains unclear.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Follow one customer or internal request from start to finish. Remove one handoff that adds delay without adding a useful check.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to name the people affected by a problem and the people able to change it. Compare the two lists before proposing solutions.
