> A growing system stays trustworthy when capability, support, and review remain visible.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn to name the human skill, support structure, and review measure a growing system must protect.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Practical frame
> Before scaling a tool or program, identify the human capability, the institutional support, the decision owner, and the evidence that triggers review.

Four sources show why output alone can hide the skills and support behind it. NASA sets out public coverage for complex mission work. MIT Sloan asks what happens when people use AI in place of their own thinking. Kauffman's BCSI profile describes the staff and systems needed to support a workforce pathway. Federal Reserve data pair household gains with signs of financial strain.

These sources do not prove one common cause. They suggest a practical check: name the skill people need to keep, the support they need, who owns the decision, and when to review it.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Map one complex system. Name its owner, checkpoint, human skill, and evidence that would change the plan.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Decide what reasoning must stay in human hands, what support lets people practice it, and what measure triggers review.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Pair a learning or workforce outcome with the staffing, systems, and burden measure needed to make the pathway usable.
