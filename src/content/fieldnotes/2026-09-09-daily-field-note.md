> [!quote] Field Note
> Ambitious plans become more credible when the system around them is visible and its failure modes can be tested.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn how to connect coordination, designed practice, and failure checks before scaling a plan.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Build the system, then test its failure modes
> Define the desired outcome, map the handoffs that carry it, then ask how the plan could fail before adding more effort.

The World Bank Group describes a 50-million-person electricity-access milestone under Mission 300 through country plans, policy reform, financing, and partners. This shows how coordination can turn a public goal into operating capacity. It does not prove that every project will succeed or that access will be sustained.

The same pattern appears in teaching and business. AACSB lists a workshop on effective case teaching, which shows deliberate attention to practice but not improved learning. For a cross-border business, check which goods a tariff covers, when it takes effect, and who bears the cost. A headline total alone cannot establish the effect on one firm.

Farnam Street's inversion model adds a useful check. Ask what would make the plan fail, then remove the few avoidable conditions before scaling effort. This is a decision practice, not evidence that inversion guarantees better judgment. The practical move is to make the system visible, test the handoffs, and name the limits of the evidence.

In a failure rehearsal, say when the team should pause to learn and when it must execute a known procedure. Make the transition between those goals explicit.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Inversion and The Power of Avoiding Stupidity](https://fs.blog/inversion/)
> **Useful idea:** Examine the opposite of the desired outcome and avoid actions that would predictably undermine it.
> **Short quote:** “Avoiding stupidity is easier than seeking brilliance.”
> **Use today:** Write the failure version of one plan before deciding what to scale.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Separate the verified event, the proposed mechanism, and the consequence that still needs evidence.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Map the handoffs behind one important promise, then remove one avoidable failure mode before committing more resources.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Design a case around a decision, its feedback loop, and the evidence that would show whether the practice worked.

## Reading Room

[Dragoni, Hymer, Yoon, LePine, Woodruff, Young, Li (2026). Leader ambidexterity in goal orientation and team adaptive performance. Journal of Applied Psychology.](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0001405)

Field data from 79 teams at a U.S. military academy linked leaders’ ability to shift between learning and performance goals with better team adaptation. The association remained after accounting for more stable goal orientations. The study does not establish a universal leadership routine or prove causation. Its useful distinction is between a fixed priority and a priority that fits changing demands. In a failure rehearsal, say when the team should pause to learn and when it must execute a known procedure. Make the transition between those goals explicit. This is an abstract-based reading; the suggested practice is a proposed application.
