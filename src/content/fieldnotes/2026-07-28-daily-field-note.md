## The Field Note

> [!example] Check the Map
> Before you act on a plan, ask who closest to the work would correct it.

Today's sources were different, but they shared one pattern.

A small-business plan must remove real friction, not only list support. A skills report must connect courses to occupations. A career break works best when it is planned before the breaking point. A giving strategy needs local donor feedback. A trade tool must match the capacity of small firms.

Each source is a reminder that the map is not the terrain. A plan, dashboard, report, or framework can help. But it becomes safer when someone close to the work can say, "This part does not match what we see."

Use this check before you act:

1. **Map:** What does the plan say should happen?
2. **Terrain:** What does the person closest to the work see?
3. **Correction:** What should change before people depend on it?

This does not prove that one policy, learning path, rest strategy, giving model, or trade tool works everywhere. It does show a better habit: check the map before the map starts making decisions for people.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool: The Map Is Not the Territory
> **Mental model:** [The Map Is Not the Territory](https://fs.blog/map-and-territory/)  
> **Useful idea:** A model helps only when people keep checking it against reality.  
> **Short quote:** "The map of reality is not reality."  
> **Use today:** Ask who has tested the plan against the real work.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> These sources do not show that the same plan, policy, break, donor strategy, or trade tool works in every setting.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> When you study a model, ask what real job, skill, or community problem it helps someone handle.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Test one dashboard, policy, or support program with a person who sees the work first.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask learners to compare official plans with lived constraints before giving advice.
