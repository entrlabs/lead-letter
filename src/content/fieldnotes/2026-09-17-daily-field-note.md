> [!quote] Field Note
> A growth plan becomes useful when people can access the capacity, understand the mechanism, measure the result, and own the next decision.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Learn how to test whether a growth plan can be used, measured, and owned by the people expected to carry it forward.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Make capacity usable
> When a plan promises growth, do not stop at the input. Ask who can use the capital, space, relationship, or job opportunity. Then name the mechanism, measure, and person who owns the next decision.

The World Bank Group’s same-day headline announces record private-capital mobilization for developing countries and connects it to job creation. AACSB describes a small business school widening its revenue and enrollment routes through alumni, community partners, credentials, and flexible pathways. Inside Higher Ed’s current business page flags wasted campus space as a planning problem. BLS reports that August job growth varied by industry, with gains in food services, local government education, and manufacturing while information employment fell.

Together, these sources support a practical discipline: make capacity usable. Capital announced is not capital deployed. A partnership named is not a working route. Available space is not useful capacity. Aggregate job growth is not an individual outcome. Identify the constraint, name the mechanism, define the measure, and assign a human owner before treating a plan as progress. The sources do not prove the World Bank’s realized jobs, the AACSB model’s general effectiveness, institution-specific campus savings, or any individual hiring result.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool
> **Mental model:** [Watch What They Do](https://fs.blog/brain-food/september-13-2026/)  
> **Useful idea:** Observable behavior is a better test of a plan than stated intention alone.  
> **Short quote:** “I just watch what they do.”  
> **Use today:** Compare a strategy’s promise with what people can actually access, use, measure, and own.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Separate announced inputs from observed outcomes, then identify the measure and owner that connect them.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Map one growth constraint, the mechanism meant to change it, the measure, and the person accountable for the next test.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Ask whether a program changes usable access or capacity, and document what people do before claiming impact.
