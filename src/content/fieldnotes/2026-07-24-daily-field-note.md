> [!quote] Field Note
> A dashboard can be accurate and still hide the work, people, or definitions that change the decision.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Add one missing-measure check before using a dashboard to judge workload, access, or system performance.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Run the Missing-Measure Check
> Before acting on a metric, name one person, task, definition, or handoff that the number cannot show.

A workload dashboard can count completed tasks without counting the mental work required to coordinate them. A health-data system can connect records without making the information useful at the moment of care. A public-benefit estimate can change when the analyst changes the unit from an individual to a household.

None of those facts makes measurement useless. They show why precision and completeness are different.

Ask three questions:

1. What work is happening outside the recorded task?
2. Which person or household disappears inside the unit we chose?
3. Which definition or handoff would change how we read the result?

Use the answers to improve the decision, not to dismiss the data. A number is most useful when the people using it can state both what it shows and what it leaves out.

The sources do not provide one universal measurement method, and they do not show that every dashboard is wrong. They support a narrower habit: make the blind spot visible before the metric becomes a judgment.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool: Inversion
> **Mental model:** [Inversion](https://fs.blog/inversion/)  
> **Useful idea:** Work backward from the conditions that would make a measurement or handoff fail.  
> **Short quote:** “Invert, always invert.”  
> **Use today:** List what a dashboard would have to omit to mislead you, then check those omissions first.

> [!note] Why It Matters
> A missing person, task, definition, or handoff can change the decision even when the recorded number is accurate.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Put one sentence beside your chosen statistic explaining what it cannot show and whose experience may be missing.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Ask who carries coordination or mental work that your workload report cannot count, then test one way to make that load visible.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Require the unit, population, and evidence limit beside every headline number before learners recommend an action.
