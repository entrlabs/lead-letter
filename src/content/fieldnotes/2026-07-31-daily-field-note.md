> [!quote] Field Note
> More effort helps only after you find what is actually blocking progress.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Pause before adding effort and name the bottleneck that actually blocks progress.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Name the Blocker
> Before asking people to push harder, write the constraint, the evidence for it, and the next test.

It is easy to ask for more effort.

Study harder. Sell harder. Train harder. Manage harder.

Sometimes that is the right answer. Often it is not the first answer.

Today's sources point to a quieter test: find the constraint first.

Inside Higher Ed highlighted colleges redesigning calculus because a gateway course can block STEM persistence. The question is not whether students should learn hard material. They should. The question is whether the course design helps them learn it or filters them out too early.

NFIB's June small-business evidence showed better expectations, but inflation and uncertainty still limited action. A founder cannot solve every pressure with optimism. The constraint may be pricing, cash, labor, suppliers, or demand.

The World Bank's education and skills work makes the same point globally. School access matters, but students also need learning, job-relevant skills, industry links, placement support, and chances to keep learning.

MIT Sloan's meritocracy example adds the management version. A system can sound fair and still produce unequal outcomes. The constraint may be hidden in evaluation rules, data, bonuses, or manager discretion.

The useful move is simple. Before you ask for more effort, name the real blocker.

Then make the next test small enough that someone can inspect it.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool: Circle of Competence
> **Mental model:** [Circle of Competence](https://fs.blog/circle-of-competence/)  
> **Useful idea:** Know the boundary of your judgment before you redesign or scale a system.  
> **Short quote:** "Know your circle of competence."  
> **Use today:** Write what you know directly, what you are guessing, and whose evidence you need.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> These sources do not prove one universal bottleneck or one universal fix for every course, firm, education system, or workplace. They show why effort should follow constraint diagnosis.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> When a class or project feels stuck, ask whether the blocker is effort, background knowledge, unclear instructions, time, or support.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Before asking a team to push harder, name one piece of friction you can remove this week.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Help learners separate "I am not capable" from "the path is badly designed or undersupported."
