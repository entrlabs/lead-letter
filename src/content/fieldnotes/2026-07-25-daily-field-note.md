> [!quote] Field Note
> When behavior keeps failing, inspect the system that rewards, permits, or overloads it before blaming the person.

> [!summary] The Reader Promise
> Use a four-part check to separate harmful intent from bad rules, weak capacity, and hidden pressure.

## The Field Note

> [!example] Diagnose Before You Respond
> Before choosing punishment, training, or redesign, inspect intent, the rule, the incentive, and the person’s capacity to comply.

Large companies can publish responsible-business policies without carrying them into purchasing decisions. A rule can be broken for personal gain, but also under pressure, from helpful intent, or because the rule itself is defective. An inclusion policy can exist while a school lacks the resources and conditions to make inclusion work.

Repeated behavior is information about both the person and the system.

Before responding, use a four-part check:

1. **Intent:** What evidence shows deliberate harm, neglect, or a good-faith mistake?
2. **Rule:** Is the expectation clear, useful, and possible to follow?
3. **Incentive:** What does the operating system reward or pressure people to do?
4. **Capacity:** Do people have the time, skill, tools, and support the expectation requires?

The check does not erase accountability. Evidence of deliberate harm still requires a direct response. The point is to avoid treating punishment or retraining as a complete fix when the same conditions will reproduce the failure.

Diagnose first, then choose the response that fits the cause: accountability, clarification, redesign, support, or a combination.

## Mental Model Lens

> [!tip] Thinking Tool: Hanlon’s Razor
> **Mental model:** [Hanlon’s Razor](https://fs.blog/mental-model-hanlons-razor/)  
> **Useful idea:** Do not assign harmful intent before checking neglect, unclear expectations, weak capacity, or bad incentives.  
> **Short quote:** “Never attribute to malice what neglect can explain.”  
> **Use today:** Ask what condition made the behavior likely before deciding how to correct it.

> [!note] Evidence Limit
> System conditions do not explain every harmful act or remove individual accountability.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> When a project fails, ask whether the cause was missing skill, missing support, a poor rule, or a deliberate choice before recommending a fix.

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> Take one repeated behavior problem through the intent-rule-incentive-capacity check before adding punishment or another training session.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Test whether the learning environment can support the behavior you require, then separate an access problem from an accountability problem.
