## The Field Note

After the November 21 earthquake in Peru, the Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System listed a magnitude of 6.7 and issued a green alert. Its model estimated that about 2,000 people could have felt strong shaking. That estimate helps a response team decide where to look first. It cannot tell a school director whether the route to campus is open.

Suppose your team has a partner in the affected area. Before marking the incident closed, ask the partner one question they can answer directly. Can staff reach the site? Record when they checked. If nobody has heard back, leave that part of the update open.

A security notice calls for a different kind of check. CISA added four entries to its catalog of known exploited vulnerabilities on November 20. Their presence on the list gives a service owner a reason to check which products the team uses. The listing alone cannot tell the owner whether a particular system was compromised. In both cases, the source tells you something precise. Make the next call about the part it leaves open.

> [!example] Try this
> Write two lines at the top of an incident update. In the first, state what the source observed. In the second, name the local fact you still need.

## Use It Today

> [!question] For Students
> Read an alert and underline one estimate. What observation would allow you to replace it with a measured result?

> [!question] For Managers And Founders
> If your incident dashboard turns green tonight, who will still be waiting for a call? Give that call an owner.

> [!question] For Educators And Advisors
> Give students a public notice. Can they explain what the issuer knows and which question belongs with a local source?

## Reading Room

**Paper:** Enrico Rubolino (2026), “Growing the Civic Mind: Civic Education, Civic Behavior, and Political Institutions,” CESifo Working Papers. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.65864/k8kouyidct).

What would count as evidence that a civics lesson mattered after class? Based on the abstract, Rubolino studied an Italian school program on fiscal responsibility. He reports greater motivation to follow rules among exposed students and higher later voter turnout in municipalities exposed to the program. These outcomes concern different groups at different times. The abstract does not give enough detail to assess the size of the effects or whether they would carry over to another country. Ask students to choose one measure of individual learning and one measure of later civic participation. What could each tell them?
